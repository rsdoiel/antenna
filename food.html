Parsnips fit naturally into this movement, offering excellent flavor while requiring relatively few imported inputs. Chefs have also embraced the vegetable’s versatility. Parsnip crisps, roasted parsnip purées, creamy soups, and even desserts occasionally appear on modern menus, demonstrating that this humble root vegetable has far more to offer than many people realize. Growing Parsnips in Ireland Ireland’s cool climate remains well suited to growing parsnips. They prefer deep, well-drained soil and benefit from a long growing season, often remaining in the ground for many months before harvest. Many gardeners intentionally leave some parsnips in the soil until late autumn or winter, allowing colder temperatures to improve their sweetness naturally. Because they store well after harvesting, parsnips continue to be a practical crop for gardeners seeking fresh produce throughout the colder months. Ingredients 1 whole guinea fowl (about 3 lbs) 2 tablespoons softened butter 1 teaspoon sea salt ½ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper 2 sprigs fresh thyme 2 sprigs fresh rosemary 2 cloves garlic, lightly crushed 1 tablespoon olive oil For the Whiskey Jus 1 shallot, finely diced 1 tablespoon butter ¼ cup Irish whiskey 1½ cups chicken stock 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard 1 teaspoon honey 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon cold water (optional) Instructions Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C).
