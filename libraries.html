July 2026 board meeting summary

2026-09-17

We’re providing a summary of the board’s July 2026 meeting. The July board meeting is our only in-person meeting. The board gathers for two days of strategic thinking and long-range planning, as well as conducting annual board business. This year, we focused on allocating surplus funds we have on hand to invest in a three-year project to redesign the content system. In a related discussion, we decided to lower content registration fees starting in 2027 because we’ve had a demonstrated history of generating surpluses, exceeding what we need to run Crossref. We also discussed the board structure, as part of our regular review of stakeholder governance. And we conducted annual board business, such as approving the audited financial statements and reviewed our risk register and mitigation plans.

The resolutions are available on the dedicated section of our website, which also lists the members of the Board and offers further information about our governance.

We also managed to squeeze in watching a World Cup game over dinner.

Board business

July board meeting agenda

The board voted unanimously to approve the agenda for the July 2026 meeting. The board then voted to approve the consent agenda, which includes a package of routine board business items.

March board minutes

The board voted unanimously to approve the minutes of the March 2026 board meeting.

Vote to approve a board policy on applying to staff roles

The board discussed and approved a Board Policy to define how to handle instances in which Crossref Board members wish to apply for open Crossref staff positions. The proposed policy applies Crossref’s Conflict of Interest Policy. Board members will be required to step down from the Board if they are applying for a position on the Senior Management or Executive Teams.

Audit Committee report

The audit committee presented the 2025 audited financial statements for review and approval. Crossref received a clean opinion and is in a healthy financial position. The board unanimously approved the statements, which are now available on the finance page of our website

Team & operational updates

Ed Pentz, Ginny Hendricks, and Lucy Ofiesh presented organisational updates. This includes updates on our midyear financial performance, progress on goals for the year, and a review of member participation and statistics on the metadata records and relationships captured in our State of Research Nexus report.

We also provided an update on progress towards establishing a second legal entity, an AISBL in Belgium.

Each July, the board reviews the risk register and risk mitigation plans prepared by the Senior Management Team.

Time-limited investment in our system redesign project

The executive team presented a proposal to allocate $4.9 million from the cash reserves generated through several years of surpluses to hire additional staff for three years to join the permanent team in redesigning the Crossref system (CS). The overall approach is to shrink Crossref’s monolithic code base by gradually rewriting individual modules, with the ultimate goal of delivering more flexible tools and services and strengthening the long-term reliability of the Crossref system. The board discussed the proposal. Following an active discussion on the approach to the redesign and the responsible use of surplus funds, the board approved the use of up to $1.6 million per year for three years.

For more information on the redesign project, please see our recent blog post, Investing $4.9 million of our surplus in rebuilding the Crossref system.

Strategic positioning

We brought in a consultant to help lead the board in a discussion of our long-range strategic position. The board discussed the challenges and opportunities facing scholarly communication, Crossref’s current position in the community, and the role we might play in the future as the needs of our community evolve. This discussion will inform an update to our strategic agenda and the preparation of an impact plan.

Governance review

Periodically, the board conducts a review of the governing structure. These reviews coincide with our biannual POSI self-assessments.

This year’s review was taken in two parts: the consideration of augmenting our governance structure by adding an Advisory Board, and reviewing how we allocate seats on the board. We discussed both of these at length and, while not yet making any changes, the board recognized the need to continue to review this periodically.

Proposal to lower content registration fees

The board discussed the ongoing work of the project known as Resourcing Crossref for Future Sustainability (RCFS). This multi-year effort, which began in 2023, aims to make our fees more accessible, equitable, and simpler to understand and operate.

Phase 1 of RCFS focused primarily on lowering membership fees for the least resourced members and simplifying underused, complex content registration fees. In January 2026, we rolled out a new, lower membership fee, removed little-used discounts that contributed to fee complexity, extended our GEM program to include zero-fee membership and content registration for 18 additional countries, and introduced a two-year fee waiver on back-year grants.

Phase 2 of RCFS focused on lowering and further simplifying content registration fees. At the July meeting, the board reviewed and discussed a proposal to (1) reduce content registration fees from $1.00 to $0.95 and from $0.25 to $0.20, while leaving $0.06 record types unchanged, and (2) remove all fees for “back-year” records, effective January 1, 2027.

While this change will have a material financial impact, projected at approximately -760,000 USD lost revenue in 2027, Crossref is in a strong financial position and growing steadily through new members and new record growth.

The board discussed the proposal in detail and voted to approve.

More information on the proposal can be found in our recent blog post, Fee update: lowering and removing DOI registration fees in January 2027

Any other business & executive session

We close each meeting with any other business a board member wants to raise. We summarised the decisions and actions from the meeting. The board then went into Executive Session to discuss any closed issues.

There being no other business, the meeting was adjourned.

Improving how funding connects to research outputs

2026-09-15

Starting today, funder names deposited in Crossref metadata without an identifier will be matched to ROR IDs, to support an open and connected Research Nexus. Funding information is an increasingly important part of the scholarly record. The improved matching strategy supports researchers, funders, institutions, and meta-researchers, who rely on that information to understand how research is supported and what outcomes that support produces.

We start matching funder names in research outputs to ROR IDs, while we also implement our improved matching strategy. Previously deposited metadata records will remain unchanged. Identifiers from the Open Funder Registry will remain in metadata records and will not be replaced by ROR IDs.

Member-deposited metadata in Crossref is enriched in a number of ways that give useful context to the community. Earlier this year, we shared that we will be initiating a number of matching tasks to highlight relationships between works, individuals, institutions, and actions. Subsequently, we shared our strategy for matching funders to Research Organization Registry (ROR) IDs, which is far more precise than the one we’re replacing.

With this approach, we will be designing an open and transparent system, enabling greater control over the matching logic that can continue to evolve alongside community needs.

– Dominika Tkaczyk, Director of Technology at Crossref

Following extensive testing and evaluation, the new funder matching strategy is ready, and has been shown to achieve 99% precision. Starting today, every new or updated record containing funding information without a funder identifier will go through the updated matching process. Any matched ROR IDs will become part of the enriched metadata, freely available through our REST API. Matching to ROR identifiers means that we are continuing our commitment to using and supporting open, community-governed infrastructure.

Improved funder matching creates stronger links between funding and research outcomes, helping build a more complete picture of how research is supported and connected in the Research Nexus. As we argue in our recent position paper on PIDs in research infrastructure policy, the value of a Crossref DOI comes from the rich, open metadata, relationships, services and stewardship built on top of it. Funder matching puts that principle into practice: adding a ROR ID creates a more reliable connection between research outputs and the organisations that fund them.

What does this mean?

Funder names deposited without an identifier will be matched to ROR IDs, where a match is found. During our mid-year community call held on 13 May 2026, we demonstrated how we would match funder IDs to ROR IDs. Crossref Director of Technology, Dominika Tkaczyk, demonstrated four cases of funder matching—from straightforward to more complex matching scenarios, with increasing discrepancies between the input and the name variant in the ROR registry. While our strategy successfully resolved these discrepancies, it also doesn’t overmatch and won’t return a result for a generic name, such as “Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences”.

Watch the demo:

No matching outcome is perfect, as you may have read in our evaluation process. The new method builds in a way to remove or replace incorrect matches, which is a feature that has been missing from our previous matching implementation.

Why open funding metadata matters

As the research ecosystem continues to embrace the principles of Open Science, it is important that the metadata associated with research funding is openly available. It increases the transparency of funding sources, supporting compliance with their funder requirements and research integrity. For funders, identifying the outcomes of funded research helps track the impact of their grants.

This work also reflects our commitment to the Barcelona Declaration on Open Research Information, which calls for open research information to support transparency, accountability, and reuse. Funder matching addresses at least one of the barriers to improving the quality and flow of funding metadata as reflected in a recently published action plan.

One matching project down, more to go

Funder matching is one of a series of projects in our metadata enrichment journey. As we continue developing methods to match metadata, each enhancement will help to strengthen the connections that underpin the research nexus. Next up we will be matching grant numbers and funder identifiers to grant DOIs registered with Crossref.

Questions you might have

Will my existing record change?

No. Existing records remain unchanged unless you update them.

What if Crossref makes an incorrect match?

Let us know, we’ll investigate and improve the matching.

Can I already deposit ROR IDs?

Yes.

What if my funder doesn’t have a ROR ID?

If no corresponding ROR ID exists, no ROR ID will be added.

Strengthening Scholarly Publishing in Africa

2026-09-14

French version –– Version française

Strengthening Scholarly Publishing in Africa is a collaborative webinar series bringing together ASSAf, Crossref, DOAJ, EIFL, and PKP to share practical guidance, resources, and approaches for strengthening the publishing ecosystem in the region. This series responds to the growing interest in open access, digital publishing, stronger editorial practices, and greater visibility of research publishing across Africa. Each partner organisation has long experience of building that capacity in the area of their expertise. In this series, we’re coming together to offer a more holistic learning experience to the community of African publishers, putting different aspects of the publishing process in their broader context.

The entire series is offered simultaneously in English and French.

You are welcome to join any of the sessions selectively, or participate actively in the full series to receive a certificate of completion.

Please read on about the learning objectives for each webinar and register for the sessions of interest.

30 September 2026: Strengthening Trusted Open Access Publishing in Africa

This opening session introduces the purpose and structure of the webinar series, setting the scene for a coordinated capacity-building pathway to strengthen scholarly publishing in Africa. The session will highlight why editorial quality, transparency, publishing standards, discoverability, and sustainability are essential for trusted open access journals and book publishing. It will also explain how participation in open scholarly infrastructure, including DOAJ, OJS, Crossref, and related services, supports the visibility and integrity of African research. Participants will be introduced to the main themes of the series, including digitisation and preservation, publishing platforms, editorial workflows, metadata, research visibility, and long-term journal sustainability. The session will position the series as a collaborative effort to support African publishers in improving quality, strengthening operations, and connecting their publications more effectively to the global scholarly communication ecosystem.Register

7 October 2026: Day-to-day journal operations

Efficient editorial workflows are crucial for maintaining quality and timely publication. This session explores ways to optimise daily editorial workflows from manuscript submission, through editorial review, selection of reviewers, peer review, production, publication and post-publication discussions. Special attention will be paid to the realities of volunteer-led and institutionally supported journals, as well as to common areas for improvement in journal transparency, discoverability, accessibility, interoperability and compliance with good publishing practices, and to approaches for systematically assessing journals against these requirements. This session builds on the EIFL Guide on Editorial Workflows in Diamond Open Access Journals, but will also be useful to the editors of all types of journals.Register

21 October 2026: Plug It In: Connecting OJS to Scholarly Infrastructure

This session introduces the key settings, workflows, and metadata practices that help journals connect OJS to the wider scholarly communications ecosystem. Participants will learn where metadata is entered and managed in OJS, how to configure policies and settings that support indexing services such as DOAJ, and how to use built-in features and plugins to connect with services like Crossref. The session will also highlight the importance of maintaining these connections through regular platform upgrades and good metadata stewardship.Register

4 November 2026: Sustainability of scholarly publishing in Africa

Sustainability is a multidimensional construct that goes beyond financial resources to include human, technological, and social capital ( Kwanya and Ateka (2026)). This session explores journal sustainability, drawing from experiences of African Diamond OA journals developing and implementing their sustainability strategies and action plans. Guidance will be provided on how to shift from issue-to-issue or year-to-year planning to multi-year planning and from rough cost estimates to clearly defined funding streams with realistic expectations and a contingency plan. Common funding challenges will be discussed and practical solutions shared. Financial Sustainability Planning Toolkit for Diamond OA journals with planning templates will be presented, and different funding sources discussed (based on Funding sources of African no-fee open access journals).Register

25 November 2026: Enhancing research visibility and trustworthiness through metadata

Research institutions across Africa produce valuable scholarly outputs, yet gaps in metadata practices can limit their visibility, discoverability, trustworthiness, and connection to the global research ecosystem. This session will offer practical tips about workflows to support registering richer metadata for increased research visibility and impact.Register

The organisations behind the series

ASSAf

The Academy of Science of South Africa (ASSAf) was inaugurated in May 1996 by the former President of South Africa and patron of the Academy, Nelson Mandela. It was formed in response to the need for an academy of science congruent with the dawn of democracy in South Africa – activist in its mission of using science for the benefit of society. The mandate of the Academy encompasses all fields of scientific enquiry and it includes the full diversity of South Africa’s distinguished scientists. The Parliament of South Africa passed the Academy of Science of South Africa Act (Act 67 of 2001), as amended, which came into operation in May 2002. ASSAf is the official national Academy of Science of South Africa and represents the country in the international community of science academies. Since its inception, ASSAf has grown from a small, emergent organisation to a well-established academy.

Crossref

Crossref is a not-for-profit membership organisation that runs open infrastructure for a connected scholarly research record. We connect 25,000+ members across 167 countries, registering over 185 million records and facilitating 2.1 billion metadata queries each month. Our mission is to make research objects easy to find, cite, link, assess, and reuse.

DOAJ

The Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) is a unique and extensive index of diverse open access journals from around the world, driven by a growing community. It is committed to ensuring quality content is openly available online for everyone. DOAJ is run and managed by the DOAJ Foundation, a non-profit community-governed Danish foundation based in Denmark, but has team members and Ambassadors from all over the world. DOAJ’s mission is to increase the visibility, accessibility, reputation, usage and impact of quality, peer-reviewed, open access scholarly research journals globally, regardless of discipline, geography or language.

EIFL

EIFL (Electronic Information for Libraries) is a not-for-profit organisation that works with libraries to enable access to knowledge in developing and transition economy countries in Africa, Asia Pacific, Europe and Latin America.

PKP

The Public Knowledge Project (PKP) is a not-for-profit, community-governed organization based at Simon Fraser University (SFU), with its remote team based all around the world. Its vision is for a world where everyone can access and meaningfully contribute to open knowledge for the benefit of all. In collaboration with our global community, PKP conducts research, develops free and open source software, and works to increase the quality, diversity and accessibility of scholarly publishing. While it is best known for Open Journal Systems (OJS) used by more than 60,000 journals globally, PKP also develops Open Monograph Press (OMP) and Open Preprint Systems (OPS).

30 septembre 2026 : Renforcer l’édition en libre accès de confiance en Afrique

Les participants découvriront les principaux thèmes de la série, notamment la numérisation et la conservation, les plateformes de publication, les flux de travail éditoriaux, les métadonnées, la visibilité de la recherche et la pérennité à long terme des revues. Cette session présentera la série comme une initiative collaborative visant à aider les éditeurs africains à améliorer la qualité, à renforcer leurs opérations et à relier plus efficacement leurs publications à l’écosystème mondial de la communication scientifique.S’inscrire

7 octobre 2026 : Le fonctionnement quotidien des revues

Des flux de travail éditoriaux efficaces sont essentiels pour maintenir la qualité et assurer une publication dans les délais. Une attention particulière sera accordée aux réalités des revues gérées par des bénévoles et soutenues par des institutions, ainsi qu’aux domaines communs à améliorer en matière de transparence, de découvrabilité, d’accessibilité, d’interopérabilité et de conformité aux bonnes pratiques éditoriales, et aux approches permettant d’évaluer systématiquement les revues au regard de ces exigences. Les organisations à l’origine de la série

ASSAf

L’Académie des sciences d’Afrique du Sud (ASSAf) a été inaugurée en mai 1996 par l’ancien président de l’Afrique du Sud et parrain de l’Académie, Nelson Mandela. Elle a été créée pour répondre au besoin d’une académie des sciences en phase avec l’avènement de la démocratie en Afrique du Sud – une académie engagée dans sa mission consistant à mettre la science au service de la société. Le mandat de l’Académie englobe tous les domaines de la recherche scientifique et rassemble toute la diversité des éminents scientifiques sud-africains. Le Parlement sud-africain a adopté la loi sur l’Académie des sciences d’Afrique du Sud (loi n° 67 de 2001), telle que modifiée, qui est entrée en vigueur en mai 2002. L’ASSAf est l’Académie nationale officielle des sciences d’Afrique du Sud et représente le pays au sein de la communauté internationale des académies des sciences. Depuis sa création, l’ASSAf est passée d’une petite organisation naissante à une académie bien établie.

Crossref

Crossref est une organisation associative à but non lucratif qui gère une infrastructure ouverte destinée à relier les données issues de la recherche scientifique. Nous mettons en relation plus de 25 000 membres répartis dans 167 pays, enregistrons plus de 185 millions d’entrées et traitons 2,1 milliards de requêtes de métadonnées chaque mois. Notre mission est de faciliter la recherche, la citation, la mise en lien, l’évaluation et la réutilisation des objets de recherche.

DOAJ

Le Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) est un index unique et exhaustif regroupant une grande diversité de revues en libre accès du monde entier, animé par une communauté en pleine expansion. Il s’engage à garantir que des contenus de qualité soient librement accessibles en ligne pour tous. Le DOAJ est dirigé et géré par la Fondation DOAJ, une fondation danoise à but non lucratif régie par la communauté et basée au Danemark, mais qui compte des membres de l’équipe et des ambassadeurs issus du monde entier. La mission du DOAJ est d’accroître la visibilité, l’accessibilité, la réputation, l’utilisation et l’impact des revues de recherche scientifique de qualité, évaluées par des pairs et en libre accès, à l’échelle mondiale, indépendamment de la discipline, de la situation géographique ou de la langue.

EIFL

EIFL (Electronic Information for Libraries) est une organisation à but non lucratif qui collabore avec les bibliothèques pour faciliter l’accès à la connaissance dans les pays en développement et en transition économique d’Afrique, d’Asie-Pacifique, d’Europe et d’Amérique latine.

PKP

Le Public Knowledge Project (PKP) est une organisation à but non lucratif, gérée par sa communauté, basée à l’université Simon Fraser (SFU), dont l’équipe travaille à distance depuis les quatre coins du monde. Sa vision est celle d’un monde où chacun peut accéder à la connaissance ouverte et y contribuer de manière significative, pour le bien de tous. En collaboration avec sa communauté mondiale, le PKP mène des recherches, développe des logiciels libres et open source, et œuvre à améliorer la qualité, la diversité et l’accessibilité de l’édition scientifique. Bien qu’il soit surtout connu pour Open Journal Systems (OJS), utilisé par plus de 60 000 revues à travers le monde, le PKP développe également Open Monograph Press (OMP) et Open Preprint Systems (OPS).
